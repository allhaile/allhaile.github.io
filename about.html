The longer answer.

// origin

I didn't come to engineering through computer science.

I studied cognitive science at UC Berkeley — the field that sits between psychology, linguistics, neuroscience, and computer science. I was studying how humans process information long before I wrote my first component.

That background changes how I build. When I look at an interface, I'm thinking about working memory limits, about what a user has to hold in their head, about where attention naturally goes. Cognitive load isn't a buzzword to me — it's the lens.

// now

I'm a Web Experience Engineer at Slack, working on platform engineering — the developer-facing web experiences that ship alongside the product. My focus is on the systems that scale: design systems, reusable components, documentation infrastructure, and the tooling that makes other engineers faster.

// why "Haile API"

I work with APIs every day. I think about them constantly — how endpoints are designed, how responses are structured, how a well-designed API reduces cognitive load for the developer consuming it.

Your personal site is the same problem. Who's the user? What are they requesting? What should the response include?

This site is my attempt to build my identity the way I'd build an API: intentionally, with documentation, and with the human on the other end in mind.

// outside the terminal

Oakland native. Traveling to eat (21 countries, still hungry). Writing about what I'm building. Documenting the workflow so future engineers don't have to reverse-engineer it from scratch.

I also run Hungry Highly — a food, travel, and dinner series where I eat my way through cities and host dinners for people I want to learn from. It's the other half of "eat curious."
